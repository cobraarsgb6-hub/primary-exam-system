Instead of reading 36 raw PowerPoint slides, all core rules, definitions, circuit symbols, and review questions from the teacher's presentation have been organized into the 5 interactive cards below:

Concept 1 • Teacher's Slides 1 to 5 Core Foundations

What is Electrical Energy and an Electric Cell?

⚡ Electrical Energy (Slide 4)

"Electrical energy is caused by moving electric charges called electrons. The faster the electric charges move, the more electrical energy they carry."

🔋 Electric Cell (Slide 5)

"An electric cell is a device which converts chemical energy into electrical energy." It has two terminals: Positive (+) and Negative (-).

Concept 2 • Teacher's Slides 6 to 8 Circuit Types

Electric Circuit and the Role of the Switch

🟢 Closed Circuit (Closed Switch)

A closed, continuous loop with no gaps. Electric current flows freely from the cell, through the wire, and lights the bulb!

🔴 Open Circuit (Open Switch)

Not a continuous path! There is a gap in the circuit. Electric current CANNOT flow, and the bulb does not light.

Concept 3 • Teacher's Slides 9 to 11 Symbols & Materials

Conductors vs Insulators & Standard Circuit Symbols

Conductors (Slide 9):
Substances that allow electricity to pass through easily (e.g. Copper, Iron, Aluminium, Graphite).
Insulators (Slide 9):
Substances that do NOT allow electricity to pass through (e.g. Rubber, Glass, Plastic, Dry Wood).
Official Cambridge Circuit Symbols (Slides 10 & 11):
• Cell: [ + |--⋮-- − ] (One long line for +, one short thick line for −)
• Battery: Multiple cells connected together in series.
• Bulb / Lamp: Circle with a cross ⨂
• Open Switch: Circle with an angled disconnected line.
Concept 4 • Teacher's Slides 14 to 30 Core Exam Topic

Series vs Parallel Circuits: The Crucial Difference

Series Circuit (Slides 14–18)

There is only ONE single path for current to flow. All components are connected in a line.

⚠️ Disadvantage (Slide 26): If ONE bulb burns out or is removed, the entire circuit is broken and ALL other lights go out!
Parallel Circuit (Slides 19–25)

The circuit splits into two or more separate branches. Current has multiple paths to flow through.

⭐ Major Advantage (Slide 30): Used in household lighting! If one light bulb in your bedroom goes out, the living room lights STAY ON!
Concept 5 • Teacher's Review Slides 32 to 34 Self-Check Quiz

The Exact Review Questions Given by the Teacher

Slide 32 Review: "What is the name for a circuit that has only ONE path for current to flow?"
Answer: Series Circuit.
Slide 33 Review: "What happens to the lights in a series circuit if one light goes off? And why?"
Answer: All the lights go out because the circuit is broken and there is no alternative path for current!
Slide 34 Review: "What happens when one light goes out in a parallel circuit?"
Answer: The other lights stay ON because each branch has its own independent complete loop!

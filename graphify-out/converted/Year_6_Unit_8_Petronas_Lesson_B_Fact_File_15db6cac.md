Learning Goals
- Explain how the author builds tension and suspense.
- Describe how Baba changes, using evidence from the story.
- Retell the escape from the city in the correct order.
Four Ways Authors Build Tension
| Technique | How it works | Example from the lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short sentences | Make the action feel clipped and the silence feel intense. | “No bird sang. No breeze blew.” |
| Ordinary things turn strange | Make familiar things seem alive or threatening. | “Even the flowers seemed to mock her.” |
| Time running out | Creates urgency because characters must act quickly. | The people must escape as danger grows. |
| Fear in dialogue | Whispers, interruptions and warnings show fear. | Baba whispers, “Petronas – it doesn’t want us to leave.” |

Important Language Choices
| Quotation | Technique / choice | Effect on the reader |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “No bird sang. No breeze blew.” | Short sentences; emphasis on silence | Creates an eerie, tense mood and suggests something is wrong. |
| “Even the flowers seemed to mock her.” | Personification | The flowers seem alive and cruel; the city feels threatening. |
| “A long, hissing shadow raced towards them” | Powerful verb and sound word | Makes the danger seem fast and frightening. |
| “too perfect” | Careful word choice | Suggests that the city’s beauty is suspicious rather than safe. |
| “The stick started bucking in Baba’s hands.” | Powerful verb | Suggests the stick moves wildly, like a powerful animal. |



Baba’s Character Development
| Earlier in the story | In pages 92–130 | What the change shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proud; wants to prove she is a good leader. | Blames herself: “it was all her fault”. | She recognises that her decisions may have put others in danger. |
| Dismisses her mother’s story as superstition. | Stays by her mother and fears for her. | She becomes more willing to listen and care. |
| Tells the people to stay in the city. | Tells the people to leave: “Get everybody out!” | She acts protectively and takes responsibility. |

Key Details to Remember
| Detail | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| The flowers lean towards Baba and Tulsi. | This strange behaviour suggests the city may be watching or listening. |
| A humming sounds like a bakar being tuned when nobody is there. | An unexplained sound adds mystery and suspense. |
| The door disappears and only solid stone remains. | The people are trapped; the city seems to prevent their escape. |
| The wall is too high to haul the rotobeasts over. | The group must find another way out and cannot leave their animals behind. |
| Baba says, “Fight magic with magic.” | She decides to use the water stick against the city’s magic. |
| “then there will be water” | This phrase suggests hope for the future. |

Remember
A strong reading answer makes a clear point, uses accurate evidence from the book, and explains how the evidence supports the idea.

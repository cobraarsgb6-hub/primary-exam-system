# Fact File: Biographies
A biography is a record of the events in a person’s life, written from the viewpoint of an author. This file defines its features, the language of point of view, and the key words from the lesson.
## Features of a biography
| Feature | Definition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening statement | The first sentence. It tells the reader who the text is about. | ‘Samantha Cristoforetti is an ESA astronaut.’ |
| Third person | The writer tells the story from outside, using he, she, they and the person’s name. | She was born in Milan, Italy. |
| Chronological order | Events are written in the order they happened. | 2001 → 2009 → 2014 → 2015 → future plans |
| Dates and facts | Real details that can be checked: dates, places, numbers. | 26 April 1977 · 200 days in space |
| Paragraphs and sections | Each paragraph or heading covers one part of the person’s life. | Personal background, Education, Training |
| Pictures and captions | A photograph shows the person. A caption explains the picture. | A photograph of Samantha in her flight suit |
| Direct speech | The person’s exact words, written inside quotation marks. | ‘The sky is not the limit.’ |
## Other features of the language
- Past tense: used for events that have already happened (was born, graduated, launched). Future tense appears only under “Future plans”.
- Formal language: serious, careful vocabulary rather than chatty words (conducted experiments, was assigned).
- Facts: statements that can be checked and proved. A biography uses mostly facts because its purpose is to inform.
- Opinion: what someone thinks or feels. It cannot be proved, e.g. ‘Samantha is an inspiration.’
## Biography or autobiography?
|  | Biography | Autobiography |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definition | The story of someone’s life, written by another person | The story of someone’s life, written by that person |
| Narrative | Third person | First person |
| Pronouns | he, she, it, they, him, her, them, his, hers, theirs | I, we, me, us, mine, ours |
| Language | May be positive or negative | Likely to be positive |
| Example | She was born in Milan, Italy. | I was born in Milan, Italy. |
## Point of view
Point of view is who tells the story and how the writer feels about the subject.
- Who is telling the story? Look at the pronouns: first person (I, we) or third person (he, she, they).
- How does the writer feel? Look at the word choice: positive, negative or neutral.
| Pronoun type | Job | Third person | First person |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | Does the action | he, she, it, they | I, we |
| Object | Receives the action | him, her, it, them | me, us |
| Possessive | Shows who owns it | his, hers, theirs | mine, ours |
## Reading skills
- Skim: read quickly to get the main idea. Look at the title, author, first line, headings and photograph.
- Scan: look quickly for specific details, such as keywords like ‘born’, dates and capital letters.
- Context: the words around a new word. Use them to work out its meaning.
## Key words
| Word | Meaning | Example sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| aeronautics | The science of designing, building and operating aircraft | She studied aeronautics to learn about aircraft. |
| ESA | European Space Agency | An ESA astronaut works for the European Space Agency. |
| assign | To give someone a particular job or task | The crew was assigned to fly to the ISS. |
| conduct | To carry out an activity, such as an experiment or a survey | Astronauts conduct experiments in space. |
| launch | To send a rocket or spacecraft up into space | The rocket will launch tomorrow morning. |

# Learning focus
In this lesson, we learn to describe the setting and mood, spot clues that may hint at future events, and compare how Baba and Ki view the city. Strong answers use evidence from the text.
# How the author builds a mysterious mood
- Silence and emptiness: No people or sounds can make the reader feel that something is missing.
- Warnings: A character feels uneasy or tells an old story.
- Strange details: Things appear or change when nobody seems to notice.
- Living scenery: Walls, windows or water seem to act like living things.
# Language spotlight: personification
Personification makes the city seem alive, watchful or threatening.
| Short quotation | What it means | Effect on the reader |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Blank windows gazed down” (pp. 22–23) | Windows cannot gaze. | The city seems to watch the visitors. |
| “The buildings loomed” (pp. 36–37) | Buildings seem to lean over Baba. | The city feels threatening. |
| “rippled as if with laughter” (pp. 38–39) | Water cannot really laugh. | The water seems alive, as if it knows a secret. |
# Characters and their viewpoints
| Character | What we learn |
| --- | --- |
| Baba | A young leader of a desert tribe. She is proud, wants to be remembered, and hopes to find enough water for her people to settle. She is excited by the city and pushes away doubts. |
| Tulsi | Baba’s loyal attendant. He worries a lot and stays by her side. |
| Ki | Baba’s grumpy elder uncle. He doubts the city and feels suspicious. |
| Shant | An old woman elder who trusts Baba and thinks Ki worries too much. |
| Jad | Tulsi’s father. He is practical and thinks about the water. |
| Anda | Baba’s mother. She worries about the old stories. |
Baba’s viewpoint: the city offers water and hope.                                                                             Ki’s viewpoint: the city feels wrong, and the clues should not be ignored.
